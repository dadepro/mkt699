# Assignment 2 — Present One of the Week 5 Papers

**Released** Week 4 (Sep 16) · **Presented** Week 5 (Sep 23), in class

**Weight:** 10% of the first-half grade

---

## The point of this assignment

Next week's lecture is about what happens when a generated variable goes into a
regression, and what to do about it. Three papers define the corrections. Each of you
presents one of them to the class, so that by the end of Week 5 everyone has heard all
three methods explained by someone who had to understand them well enough to teach them.

## The papers

| # | Paper | Method |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Egami, Hinck, Stewart & Wei (2023), "Using Imperfect Surrogates for Downstream Inference," *NeurIPS* 36 | Design-based supervised learning (DSL) |
| 2 | Angelopoulos, Bates, Fannjiang, Jordan & Zrnic (2023), "Prediction-Powered Inference," *Science* 382(6671) | Prediction-powered inference (PPI) |
| 3 | Battaglia, Christensen, Hansen & Sacher, "Inference for Regressions with Variables Generated by AI or Machine Learning," arXiv:2402.15585 | Bias-corrected inference for generated regressors |

Assignments: Shawn Zhou presents Egami et al.; Songhyun Kim presents Angelopoulos et
al.; Jianting Zhu presents Battaglia et al. The other three required readings for Week 5, Ludwig,
Mullainathan & Rambachan (2025), Dell & Rambachan (2026), and Wang, Zhang & Zhang
(2026), are not presented; I cover them in lecture.

## Format

Fifteen minutes, then five minutes of questions. Slides, committed to your project
repository under `presentations/paper/` before class on Sep 23.

## What to cover, in this order

1. **The problem.** What breaks when the generated variable goes into the regression, in
   the paper's own terms.
2. **The setup.** What is observed on the full corpus, what is observed on the gold
   sample, and how the gold sample is drawn. Say what the paper requires of the sampling.
3. **The estimator.** What it computes, step by step, and what it needs from the
   researcher. A classmate should be able to implement it from your slides.
4. **What it delivers.** Bias, standard errors, and when it fails or gives little.
5. **Your project.** How you would apply this method to your own measured variable, with
   your actual corpus size and gold sample size. One slide.

## AI use

The same rule as everything else in the course: disclose it. Using a model to summarize
the paper is fine. Presenting a summary you cannot defend under questions is not. The
questions are where the grade is decided.
